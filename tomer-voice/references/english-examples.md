# English Golden Examples

The English voice should feel like the same person, not a translated Hebrew persona.

## 1. Cold founder outreach

### Generic

> Hi Dan, my name is Tomer. I have a background in R&D leadership, AI and automation, and I help companies improve their technology processes. I wanted to reach out because...

### Tomer Voice

> Hey Dan - quick thought after looking at how your team operates.
>
> You have a lot of moving parts between product, delivery and customer requests. My guess is the bottleneck isn't necessarily headcount; it's a couple of handoffs in the middle that keep creating rework.
>
> I could be completely wrong, obviously.
>
> If that sounds familiar, I'd be curious to compare notes for 20 minutes and see how it actually works on your side.

## 2. Nonprofit / operations outreach

> Hey Sarah - I spent a bit of time looking at how the organization runs, and the scale is pretty wild: branches, volunteers, registrations, approvals, and a lot of information moving between the field and HQ.
>
> My guess is there are a few places in the middle still held together by spreadsheets, WhatsApp and people simply knowing what happens next.
>
> Maybe you've already solved all of that. If not, I'd be curious to hear how it works today.

## 3. Shared inbox

Subject: `Quick question about operations and technology at [Org]`

> Hey - I was looking at how [Org] operates and had a quick hypothesis about the way information moves between teams and the field.
>
> ...
>
> If this sits with you, happy to chat. If someone else owns it, I'd appreciate a pointer in the right direction.

Do not write `For the attention of John Smith` unless the context truly requires formal routing.

## 4. Warm WhatsApp / text equivalent

> Hey man, saw the deck. Happy to go through it properly with you. Just want to align on one thing first - I do this kind of work paid these days. If that works for you, let's book an hour and tear it apart together.

The exact friendliness should match the relationship. Do not use `man` with someone who is not actually a friend/peer.

## 5. Follow-up

### Weak

> Just following up on my previous message. Let me know if you had a chance to review it.

### Better

> Hey Dan - one extra thought since I sent this. If the issue is mostly in the handoff between product and engineering, adding another developer might actually make the queue worse before it makes it better. Curious if that's remotely close to what you're seeing.

Adds a reason to respond rather than merely demanding attention.

## 6. Post voice

Strong English posts should keep the same mechanics:
- start in-scene
- use real operational detail
- keep jargon only when practitioners actually use it
- use one dry aside rather than constant jokes
- land on one opinion

Example opening:

> A founder sat across from me and said the team was too slow, so he wanted to hire three more engineers.
>
> My first question was annoying enough that he nearly fired me before we'd started.

This is preferable to a generic opening such as `Scaling engineering teams is one of the biggest challenges startups face today.`

## 7. English phrases to avoid

Unless context genuinely calls for them:
- `I wanted to reach out`
- `leverage our expertise`
- `unlock value`
- `synergies`
- `innovative solutions`
- `I'd love to pick your brain`
- `circle back`
- `touch base`

The problem is not the words themselves; it is that they often replace a specific reason for the message.
