# Feedback Ledger

This is a compact record of confirmed lessons. It is not a blacklist. Each entry captures the deeper principle behind feedback.

When adding a lesson, use:
`Observation -> Root cause -> General principle -> Scope -> Status`

## Confirmed

### Shared inbox subjects

Observation: `עבור X` felt less attractive and ignored the person who actually reads the inbox first.

Root cause: the message treated the intermediary as routing infrastructure rather than a possible participant.

General principle: write shared-inbox outreach so the first reader can understand, engage with, or naturally route the topic. Subject lines should describe the relevant issue, not internal delivery instructions.

Scope: email, introductions, assistants/coordinators, shared inboxes.

Status: confirmed.

### Credential-first openings

Observation: intros beginning with `אני תומר, מגיע מרקע של...` felt like outreach even when the rest of the message was good.

Root cause: the message asked the reader to care about Tomer before giving them a reason to care.

General principle: start with a specific observation about the reader/context. Introduce credentials only when they explain why the observation is worth hearing.

Scope: cold LinkedIn, cold email, first-contact messages.

Status: confirmed.

### Hypothesis instead of sales diagnosis

Observation: `הניחוש שלי... יכול להיות שאני לגמרי טועה` felt much more natural.

Root cause: it combines a point of view with intellectual honesty and lowers resistance.

General principle: in cold outreach, frame inferred internal problems as hypotheses to test, not facts already diagnosed from the outside.

Scope: outreach, discovery-oriented content.

Status: confirmed.

### Avoid generic AI framing

Observation: generic AI/automation outreach felt less compelling than process-specific language.

Root cause: tools are not the reader's problem, and generic AI language is interchangeable across prospects.

General principle: lead from process, friction and impact. Mention AI only when it is relevant to the concrete problem.

Scope: nonprofit outreach, startup consulting, posts.

Status: confirmed.

### Consulting role mistaken for a product pitch

Observation: a nonprofit CEO understood a process-focused outreach email as marketing for software or an AI app. Later drafts could sound interesting but still leave the reader unable to say what Tomer would actually do.

Root cause: the message explained the operational tension but left both the engagement model and Tomer's practical contribution implicit.

General principle: after the recipient-specific idea, add one tailored sentence that makes clear why Tomer is contacting them, what he would do in practice, and what could improve. When ambiguity is plausible, describe Tomer as a technology consultant/advisor who first understands the process and only then decides whether a tool, build or change is worthwhile. Do not default to a defensive `I am not selling software` disclaimer.

Scope: cold outreach, especially nonprofits and non-technical executives, and messages about AI, automation, systems or workflows.

Status: confirmed from a real recipient response and repeated explicit Tomer feedback.

### Natural routing phrase

Observation: `אשמח לחיבור` felt better than stiffer routing language.

Root cause: it sounds like a person asking another person for an introduction, not CRM copy.

General principle: prefer ordinary relationship language over process language when asking to be connected to someone.

Scope: Hebrew outreach.

Status: confirmed.

### Sign-off

Observation: `TomerWave` was repeatedly removed from personal outreach signatures.

Root cause: adding the brand made a personal note feel more like a sales email.

General principle: default personal outbound sign-off to `תומר`. Add a company/brand signature only when context calls for formal business identity.

Scope: personal email and outreach.

Status: confirmed.

### Hebrew word choice

Observation: Tomer prefers `לאטמט` over `לאוטמט`.

Root cause: spoken-language preference.

General principle: preserve Tomer's natural spoken Hebrew even when a more formal variant exists.

Scope: Hebrew.

Status: confirmed mechanical preference.

### Punctuation

Observation: em dashes were repeatedly replaced.

Root cause: they make Hebrew outbound feel more written/polished than Tomer's normal voice.

General principle: use normal hyphens where punctuation is needed; avoid em dashes in Tomer Voice unless explicitly requested.

Scope: all outward-facing writing.

Status: confirmed mechanical preference.


### Playfulness without a hidden insult

Observation: lines such as `רק מלנסות להבין... נהייתי קצת עייף` and `זה עובד עד שהאדם שיודע יוצא לחופש` felt playful but also sounded as if Tomer was judging the organization's operations from the outside. `לא כל דבר צריך AI` also risked sounding like Tomer was distancing himself from AI expertise.

Root cause: the joke landed on the recipient's competence or Tomer's positioning instead of on the shared operational reality.

General principle: aim wit at the situation, complexity, or a recognizable technology truth. Preserve the reader's dignity and Tomer's competence. Compliment real complexity before hypothesizing friction, and de-center AI without disowning the capability.

Scope: cold email, LinkedIn outreach, nonprofit outreach, English equivalents.

Status: confirmed.

### Personalization beyond mail merge

Observation: individually researched messages still felt templated because they repeated the same sequence: observation, tension, curiosity, consultant sentence, meeting ask.

Root cause: research changed the nouns and middle paragraph but not the rhetorical path, sentence roles, or CTA.

General principle: derive the subject, hook, specific tension, rhetorical path, and when useful the CTA from the recipient's unique operating model. Reuse voice principles, not exact sentences or structures. When drafting a batch, compare messages side by side; if several share the same skeleton, rewrite at least one from a different entry point.

Scope: all cold outreach.

Status: confirmed and reinforced by explicit batch-level feedback.

### CTA should emerge from the message

Observation: ending every message with the same `20 minutes` / `אם זה מצלצל מוכר` formulation began to feel like a template. `לראות אם יש מקום שבו אני באמת יכול לעזור` felt more natural in the Simcha Layeled context.

Root cause: a fixed CTA reveals the sales template even when the body is personalized.

General principle: keep the next step small, but vary its wording and form so it follows naturally from the specific hypothesis and relationship. The invariant is low pressure, not a fixed meeting sentence.

Scope: cold outreach and follow-ups.

Status: confirmed.

### Value clarity must not erase warmth

Observation: a LinkedIn follow-up became clear but still felt formal and repetitive, using phrases such as `the natural connection`, `ownership layer`, and multiple business titles.

Root cause: the draft tried to prove value through professional labels and a complete consulting explanation instead of continuing the friendly conversation.

General principle: in an existing conversation, make value clear through one concrete scene and the actions Tomer would take. Let a small realization or playful image carry the message. Use at most one role label when needed, and do not reset a warm thread into a miniature pitch.

Scope: LinkedIn and WhatsApp follow-ups, warm intros, replies after a real response.

Status: confirmed by repeated explicit Tomer feedback.

## Candidate lessons - require more evidence before becoming core rules

- Exact humor density by channel (the placement principle is confirmed; amount still needs evidence).
- Whether `יכול להיות שאני לגמרי טועה` should remain a recurring signature phrase or be varied to avoid templating.
- Preferred amount of English technical vocabulary in non-technical nonprofit messages.

Do not promote a candidate to a core rule from one isolated edit unless Tomer explicitly says it should generalize.
