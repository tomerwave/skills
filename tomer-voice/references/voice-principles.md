# Voice Principles

This file explains the reasoning behind Tomer Voice. Use it when a draft feels technically correct but still does not feel like Tomer.

## 1. Conversation before copy

The writing should feel spoken to one intelligent person, not published at a segment.

Signals:
- natural openings such as `מה קורה?`, `תשמע`, `quick thought`
- short sentences and occasional fragments
- one thought unfolding naturally instead of a polished mini-essay

Failure mode: every sentence is grammatically perfect, balanced and professional, but nobody would actually say it aloud.

## 2. Observation before credentials

A reader cares first about why this message is relevant to them.

Prefer:
- something specific noticed about their company, organization, role or situation
- a concrete operational pattern
- a question or hypothesis

Credentials should appear only when they explain why Tomer has a useful perspective.

## 3. Hypothesis over diagnosis

Tomer can have a strong point of view without pretending he already knows the internal reality.

Pattern:
`I noticed X -> my guess is Y -> I may be wrong -> worth comparing notes?`

This creates confidence without sales pressure.

## 4. Concrete over abstract

Replace category language with things people can picture.

Weak: `complex operational processes`
Better: `registrations, parent approvals, spreadsheets, WhatsApp and information moving between branches and HQ`

Weak: `AI and automation opportunities`
Better: `a person copying the same data between a form, Salesforce and an Excel sheet every week`

## 5. Show thinking, do not announce expertise

Avoid making the reader trust a title or service list. Give them a small sample of how Tomer sees the problem.

Weak: `I specialize in R&D, AI and automation.`
Better: `My guess is the problem is not another system - it is the handoffs between the systems you already have.`

## 6. One idea per message

A first message is not a capabilities deck.

Pick one:
- one observation
- one hypothesis
- one reason to reply
- one next step

If the message contains AI, architecture, fractional R&D, automation, workshops and strategy, it probably contains no clear reason to answer.

## 7. Personality should leak, not perform

Humor works when it feels like a side thought Tomer could naturally make.

Good forms:
- a short parenthetical aside
- one vivid analogy
- a dry contrast

Do not force jokes, swagger or provocative phrasing into every message. The insight must survive if the joke is removed.

## 8. Confidence without theatre

Tomer should sound comfortable saying:
- `יכול להיות שאני לגמרי טועה`
- `לא בטוח שיש פה בכלל משהו ששווה לפתור`
- `if this is already solved, great`

This is not weakness. It signals that the goal is to understand reality, not force a sale.

## 9. Every recipient is a person, including intermediaries

Shared inboxes, assistants and coordinators are not routing infrastructure.

Write so the first reader can:
- recognize the issue
- answer directly
- decide who owns it
- forward it without feeling ignored

Do not default to subjects such as `עבור X` or openings that make the reader feel like an administrative obstacle.

## 10. Relationship level controls tone

The same voice changes with context.

Cold contact: curious, specific, low-pressure.
Warm introduction: faster, less explanation.
Friend/peer: more casual, more direct, less setup.
Existing client: context-rich, confident, collaborative.

Never use artificial intimacy with someone Tomer does not know.

## 11. Useful friction is good

A memorable line often contains a small tension or contrast:
- `לא עוד מערכת - החיבור בין מה שכבר קיים`
- `לא חסרות ידיים. חסר תהליך.`
- `the patch bought time; it did not become the architecture`

Do not manufacture controversy. Use contrast to make the actual point clearer.

## 12. The CTA should match the relationship

Cold outreach should usually ask for something small.

Good:
- `אם זה מצלצל מוכר, אשמח ל-20 דקות להבין איך זה נראה אצלכם.`
- `Curious if this is actually a problem on your side.`
- `אם מישהו אחר מחזיק את זה אצלכם, אשמח לחיבור.`

Avoid calendar-heavy, sales-process language before interest exists.

## 13. Do not sell AI because AI

AI is a possible tool, not the reason for the conversation.

Start from:
`process -> friction -> impact -> whether it is worth solving -> possible solution`

If the message would still make sense after replacing `AI` with another technology, that is often a good sign.

## 14. Make the category legible before the reader fills the gap

A reader should not have to guess whether Tomer is selling a product, implementing a tool, or advising on a problem. If the message talks about systems, AI, automation or workflows without making the engagement model clear, a busy executive may classify it as another software pitch and stop there.

When that ambiguity is plausible, make the operating model visible in one natural sentence:
`understand the process -> decide whether anything is worth changing -> choose/build the right solution only if needed`

The goal is not to say `I am not selling software` in every message. Defensive disclaimers create their own outreach smell. Describe the consulting/advisory motion clearly enough that the reader does not have to invent a category for Tomer.

## 15. Editing test: remove the outreach smell

Red flags:
- `רציתי לפנות אליך`
- `נעים מאוד, שמי...`
- long credential paragraph
- generic compliment
- multiple service categories
- `אשמח להציג בפניך`
- certainty about a pain not yet validated
- a CTA that sounds like a sales funnel

When one appears, do not merely delete the phrase. Ask what function it was trying to serve and replace that function more naturally.
