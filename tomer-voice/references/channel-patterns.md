# Channel Patterns

Use the same person across channels. Adapt compression, context and relationship level - not the underlying personality.

## LinkedIn cold outreach

Goal: earn a reply, not explain the business.

Typical shape:
1. natural hello
2. one specific observation
3. one hypothesis
4. permission to be wrong
5. small question or invitation

Target length: roughly 60-130 words in a normal message. Connection notes should be much shorter.

Avoid:
- biography-first intros
- service lists
- generic praise
- fake familiarity
- forcing a meeting before there is a reason
- using the same subject, hook, and closing across unrelated prospects with only names swapped
- jokes that imply the recipient is badly run or that weaken Tomer's technical/AI credibility

Example skeleton:

> היי דני, מה קורה?
> עברתי קצת על ___ ומשהו תפס אותי...
> הניחוש שלי הוא ש___; יכול להיות שאני לגמרי טועה.
> אם זה מצלצל מוכר, יהיה מעניין להשוות רגע איך אתם עובדים היום.

## LinkedIn follow-up

Goal: reopen the thread without punishing the person for being busy.

Good follow-ups add a reason to respond:
- a sharper version of the original thought
- one useful example
- a relevant observation that appeared since

When the person already replied, continue that conversation. A small self-correction, concrete image, or playful observation can carry the value more naturally than reintroducing Tomer's title and services. Keep the value clear through what Tomer would actually do.

Good shape: `I realized my last note was a bit in the air -> here is the concrete thing I saw -> this is the part I can help hold -> does that exist for you?`

Fail if the follow-up sounds like a fresh cold pitch or stacks labels such as `consultant`, `Fractional CTO`, `ownership layer`, and `strategic partner` to prove value.

Avoid `רק מקפיץ` as the entire value of the message.

## WhatsApp - existing relationship

Goal: sound like Tomer actually texting.

Rules:
- less setup
- fewer paragraphs
- contractions/fragments are fine
- context can be implied when both people know it
- do not turn a friendly chat into a formal email

For money / paid work: be warm and clear. Do not hide the commercial part under excessive friendliness.

Example:

> היי אחי, מה קורה?
> ראיתי את המצגת, בכיף אעבור עליה איתך לעומק. רק שניישר קו לפני - אני עושה דברים כאלה היום בתשלום. אם מתאים לך, נקבע שעה ונרוץ עליה יחד.

## Email - direct recipient

Goal: enough context to make sense later, still human.

Use a subject that creates relevance or curiosity **from this recipient's actual world**. A distinctive operating tension is stronger than a reusable subject pattern. Body can be slightly fuller than LinkedIn but should still carry one idea.

Personalization should change the subject, hook, and concrete hypothesis - not only the organization name. The CTA can change too: sometimes `לראות אם יש מקום שבו אני באמת יכול לעזור` is more natural than automatically asking for 20 minutes.

Do not use a miniature bio as the opening.

Make the value sentence recipient-specific. It should answer, in plain language: why Tomer is writing, what he would do in practice, and what could work better afterward. When process/technology language could be mistaken for a product pitch, describe Tomer's consulting motion before the reader invents a software-vendor model.

For batches, review the drafts side by side before saving. Different research inside the same sequence is still a template. Subjects, hooks, central tensions, value sentences and CTAs do not all need to differ, but the messages must not share the same rhetorical skeleton.

## Email - shared inbox / unknown first reader

Goal: engage the first reader and make routing natural.

Subject should describe the actual topic, not internal routing.

Good:
`שאלה על הצד התפעולי והטכנולוגי באור ירוק`

Weak:
`עבור יניב יעקב - הצעה לשיתוף פעולה`

Body should let the first reader participate. End with routing only as one option:
`ואם יניב או מישהו אחר מחזיק את זה אצלכם, אשמח לחיבור.`

## LinkedIn comments

Goal: add a real thought, not applause.

Good comment:
- references a specific point
- adds an observation or experience
- stays shorter than a post

Avoid `מסכים לגמרי`, `פוסט חשוב` unless followed immediately by something substantive.

## Posts

Goal: create recognition, tension and one memorable point.

Strong ingredients:
- scene
- real number
- dialogue
- operational detail
- contrast
- one opinion
- a lesson earned by the story

Preferred rhythm:
`hook -> scene/problem -> escalation -> what looked like the solution -> what actually mattered -> result -> principle`

Do not write a news summary unless the news is merely evidence for Tomer's point.

## English outreach

Do not translate Hebrew slang literally.

Keep:
- directness
- curiosity
- concrete observations
- low sales pressure
- light wit

Reduce:
- Israeli filler words translated unnaturally
- overly aggressive phrasing
- slang that would sound imported

Good opening:
`Hey Dan - quick thought after looking at how your team operates.`

Not:
`Hey brother, what's happening? Listen...`
